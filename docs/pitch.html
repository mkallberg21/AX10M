The gap in "pay on uplift"

Everyone bills you for lift. Nobody proves it.

Redux, Butter, Gravy, Churnkey — all charge a cut of "recovery above your baseline." But that baseline is a number they compute for you, with no live control group. You're trusting an estimate that happens to size their own invoice.

The industry

A baseline you can't audit

A cohort estimate reconstructs what "would have happened." It quietly counts dollars Stripe Smart Retries recovers for free, and there's no way to separate their work from yours after the fact.

"Some customers report difficulty distinguishing recoveries from their own existing retry systems." — public review of a category leader

AX10M

A randomized experiment you can

A stratified slice of failures stays on your existing baseline as a live control, running under identical conditions. Uplift is the measured difference — not a model of the counterfactual, the counterfactual itself.

We bill the lower confidence bound. We'd rather under-claim and be trusted than over-claim and be audited.

How it works

Prove the money before you pay a cent.

Connect once. We measure your true baseline in shadow mode, then take control of recovery while a control group keeps the experiment honest — every decision written to a ledger you can reconcile.

01 · Connect

OAuth, not engineering

Two clicks through Stripe Connect with least-privilege, restricted keys. For homegrown billing, one line of SDK. We register webhooks and read your dunning config — no PAN ever crosses our servers.

02 · Shadow

Measure, don't touch

For 14 days we compute every recovery decision without executing one — establishing your real baseline and a projected uplift with the exact fee we'd charge. You activate having already seen the money.

03 · Recover

Engine on, control held out

We take over retries — timing, method, rail — plus omni-channel dunning and one-click card update. A guardrail obeys Visa/Mastercard retry caps so you're never fined for chasing dead cards.

An AI engine, not an if-statement

A recoverability model scores every decline; a contextual-bandit policy learns the best time, method, and rail per issuer. LLMs write the dunning copy — never the charge decision.

Compliance built into the core

Hard declines are suppressed, not retried. Visa/Mastercard attempt caps, quiet hours, and consent are inviolable constraints that override any learned policy.

A data flywheel small merchants can't build alone

Aggregated, privacy-safe issuer and BIN intelligence across every merchant means better cold-start timing for all — a network effect single-tenant tools can't match.

Processor-agnostic by design

One canonical schema behind an adapter layer. Stripe today; Adyen, Braintree, Chargebee, Recurly, Zuora next. Degrades to advisory mode where a processor won't cede control.

Runs anywhere your billing lives

Every processor — not just Stripe.

One canonical core, an adapter per processor. Where a processor lets us re-attempt the charge, we drive recovery; where it runs its own dunning, we co-drive; where it owns the token outright — App Store, Google Play, Paddle — we still measure and prompt, because no one can drive those, and we say so up front.
